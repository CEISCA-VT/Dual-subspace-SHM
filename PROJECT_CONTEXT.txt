===========================================================================
 PROJECT: PCA Framework — Subspace Separation for Dual-Use SHM
===========================================================================

--- PROBLEM STATEMENT ---

Piezoelectric impedance sensors (PZTs) can simultaneously measure structural
health and authenticate the sensor itself from a single impedance sweep. The
challenge is that impedance spectra mix together:
  (a) Device-specific manufacturing variation (useful for authentication)
  (b) Environmental/operational conditions (temperature, aging, loading)
  (c) Structural damage (stiffness/mass change from cracking)

Goal: Learn orthogonal subspaces such that:
  - Identity subspace encodes (a) → authenticate sensors
  - Health/condition subspace encodes (b)+(c) → detect damage
  - The two subspaces are statistically independent (low HSIC)

--- KEY INNOVATION ---

- Synthetic perturbations: Generate training data by applying controlled
  perturbations (temperature drift, aging, loading) to real baseline sweeps
  from a 295-device cohort. No real damage data needed for training.
- sisPCA (Subspace Independent PCA): Solves for projection U that maximizes
  variance while minimizing HSIC between identity and condition subspaces.
  Parameter lambda_contrast controls the trade-off.
- Cross-dataset transfer: After training on one cohort, the condition subspace
  generalizes to detect real damage on an unseen sensor from a different
  dataset (PAMELA) at 0% EER.

--- DATASETS ---

1. 01_master_dataset (cohort):
   - 295 PZT devices on aluminum substrate
   - 5 sweeps each = 1475 total sweeps
   - 10 kHz - 1 MHz, 2001 frequency points (phase + magnitude = 4002 dims)

2. PAMELA (real damage validation):
   - 1 PZT sensor, unknown structure
   - 30 sweeps: 6 temperatures (24, 40, 55, 70, 85, 100 C) x 5 damage levels
     (healthy + D1/D2/D3/D4)
   - 0 - 125 kHz, 32768 raw points (subsampled to 2000)
   - Damage is real physical cracking, not synthetic
   - Different sensor, structure, instrumentation, and frequency range than
     the cohort

--- EXPERIMENTS & RESULTS ---

EXPERIMENT 1: Method Comparison (cohort only, 295 devs)
--------------------------------------------------------
Setup: sisPCA trained on cohort (295 devs x 5 sweeps) + synthetic perturbations
  (3 temp levels, 4 aging levels, 4 load levels). PCA pre-reduction to 100 PCs.
  Evaluate authentication EER (identity subspace) and health EER (condition
  subspace). Aging levels include severity=0 ("none"), which is physically
  identical to baseline.

Key insight: The "no-none" EER excludes *none samples.

Results (health EER):
  PCA sep F-ratio:  17.3% overall, 10.0% no-none
  sisPCA λ=0.0:     15.8% overall,  0.0% no-none
  sisPCA λ=0.01:    18.8% overall,  0.0% no-none
  sisPCA λ=0.05:    15.3% overall,  0.0% no-none
  sisPCA λ=0.1+:    15-19% overall, 0.0% no-none

  Auth EER: 1.7-7.1% (higher λ = worse auth since subspace is more
  constrained)
  Temperature EER: 0.0% at all λ (temp is the strongest signal)
  Aging EER: 16-24% overall (all overlap is from aging_none)
  Loading EER: 16-24% overall (all overlap is from load_none)

Interpretation: The "health EER" of 16-24% was entirely driven by
severity=0 samples being indistinguishable from baseline. The framework
perfectly detects ANY non-zero perturbation.

EXPERIMENT 2: PAMELA Standalone
--------------------------------
Setup: 30 sweeps (6 temps x 5 damage levels). Temperature = identity
  variable (confound). Damage D1-D4 = condition to detect.
  Only 1 healthy baseline per temperature -> per-temp EER infeasible.
  EER resolution floor = 1/6 = 16.7% (only 6 healthy-damaged pairs).

Results (best: λ=10.0):
  Health EER overall: 16.7% (hits floor)
  D4 cross-validated (leave-1-temp-out): 3.3%
  Others: 10-63%

Interpretation: 30 samples is too few. sisPCA can't learn good subspaces.
The D4 cross-validated result (3.3%) shows potential but the overall
result is limited by sample size, not the method.

EXPERIMENT 3: Combined Cohort + PAMELA (KEY RESULT)
----------------------------------------------------
Setup: Add PAMELA as sensor #296. Train on cohort synthetic perturbations
  + PAMELA healthy baselines (1506 total). Evaluate on PAMELA damaged
  sweeps (24). Frequency common overlap = 10-125 kHz, 200 points.

Results (ALL λ, ALL damage levels):
  PAMELA Health EER: 0.00%
  D1 EER: 0.00%, D2: 0.00%, D3: 0.00%, D4: 0.00%
  Synth EER: 0.17-4.05% (baseline sanity check)

  Every single one of the 24 damaged PAMELA sweeps projects further from
  baseline than ALL 1506 healthy sweeps. Perfect separation.

Why it works:
  - Cohort provides 295 unique device identities -> strong identity signal
  - 1506 baselines give robust Mahalanobis distance estimation
  - Synthetic perturbations (temp/aging/load) capture general condition
    variation patterns that transfer to real damage
  - Real damage produces a stronger impedance signature than synthetic
    perturbations, so it projects even further into the condition subspace

Why standalone fails but combined succeeds:
  Standalone: 6 baselines, 1 "identity" (temperature label)
  Combined:  1506 baselines, 296 identities (295 real + 1 PAMELA)
  The rich identity signal lets sisPCA better factor out device-specific
  variation from condition-specific variation.

--- METRICS ---

Health EER: Equal Error Rate in condition subspace. Threshold where false
  accept (healthy called damaged) = false reject (damaged called healthy).
  0% = perfect, 50% = random guessing, 100% = perfectly inverted.

Auth EER: Same as above but in identity subspace. Measures if device can
  be authenticated despite condition perturbations.

HSIC: Hilbert-Schmidt Independence Criterion. Measures dependence between
  identity and condition subspace projections. Lower = more independent.
  sisPCA explicitly minimizes this. ~0 = subspaces fully disentangled.

no-none EER: Same Health EER but excludes samples tagged with severity=0
  ("none" perturbation level). These are physically identical to baseline,
  so they inflate EER artificially.

Cross-validated EER (PAMELA): Leave-one-temperature-out. Train on 5 temps,
  test on the 6th held-out temp. Tests generalization across thermal
  conditions, not fitting per-temp baselines.
